OVERVIEW

Learning bias dynamics and uncertainty.

Neural inertial odometry has demonstrated strong potential for motion estimation in challenging environments, yet inertial-only preintegration remains sensitive to IMU bias and uncertainty. PLATO is a unified framework for jointly learning IMU bias dynamics, modeled by a neural ordinary differential equation, and gyroscope and accelerometer noise covariances.

Optimization exploits the sparse structure of the negative log-likelihood. IMU noise-parameter gradients are computed by forward differentiation, while a tailored double-adjoint scheme couples a discrete invariant-error adjoint with a continuous-time adjoint for the bias model. Experiments on EuRoC and AquaLux evaluate inertial preintegration and downstream visual-inertial odometry under intermittent visual degradation.

METHOD

A unified likelihood.
Structured, efficient gradients.

Trajectory observations supervise training. The learned bias model operates on IMU inputs at deployment.

01

Trajectory observations

Ground-truth trajectories and noisy visual-odometry poses provide supervision through their observation models and assumed uncertainties.

02

Sparse marginal likelihood

Jointly learn bias dynamics and IMU noise statistics by exploiting the sparse information structure of the latent inertial trajectory.

03

Double-adjoint optimization

Couple an outer invariant-error adjoint with an inner continuous-time bias adjoint. Noise updates use forward covariance sensitivities.

Scope and limitations. PLATO is trained offline. The covariance model is isotropic; under noisy trajectory supervision, it acts as an effective likelihood weight rather than an independently calibrated physical sensor covariance. Cross-IMU transfer and out-of-distribution evaluation remain future work.
